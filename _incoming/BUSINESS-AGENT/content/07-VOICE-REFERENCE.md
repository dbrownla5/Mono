## YOUR SIGNATURE MOVES

**1. Observational specificity — name the exact real-life thing, not the abstract feeling.**
This is your #1 differentiator. You point at the concrete object/scene; you never explain the emotion.
> "the storage unit everyone avoids opening · the move that technically happened six months ago · the lightbulb that never got replaced · the unreachable shelf" `— [BRAND-VOICE.md, line 58]`
> — OBSERVATION: Your "strong vs weak" list in BRAND-VOICE.md draws this line for you: STRONG = "The closet everyone closes quickly because nobody knows what to do with it yet." WEAK = "Objects carry emotional continuity." `— [BRAND-VOICE.md, lines 63–64]`

**2. The "real-world example" / case style — you teach by telling a specific true story.**
You almost never argue a point in the abstract. You drop a real person and a real scene.
> "The 8-month pregnant woman with a beautiful baby shower, hundreds of gifts, duplicate baby gear, no time, and no energy to turn a mountain of love into something functional." `— [AWLC-SERVICES-MASTER-FINAL.md, line 204]`
> "The parent who definitely bought the iPhone but absolutely did not set up the iPhone." `— [social: IG positioning post / 06-SOCIAL-COPY.md]`

**3. Dry humor through observation — deadpan, never a joke for its own sake.**
The humor comes from noticing reality and stating it flat.
> "The storage unit started as temporary and somehow celebrated its sixth birthday." `— [social fragment]`
> "The Armani survived. A surprising number of trends did not." `— [TWLC_Master_Document.md, lines 2769–2771]`
> "Not transformed into a minimalist documentary where someone owns three bowls and a fern." `— [social: Launch Post]`
> — OBSERVATION: This is the "🦝 brave little administrative raccoon" register your memory file names. Dry stays.

**4. Short declaratives stacked, then a turn.**
Your rhythm: a stack of fragments or short lines, then one line that lands the point.
> "Just functional. / Clear. / Manageable. / Moving again." `— [social: Launch Post]`
> "Most people aren't lazy. They're overloaded." `— [BRAND-VOICE.md, line 22]`

**5. "One person instead of four" — your structural through-line.**
> "One person instead of four strangers." `— [WLC-SITE-COPY-FINAL.md, line 92]`
> "One capable person instead of four disconnected services." `— [social: Founder Post]`

**6. Remove shame, then offer relief — never pity, never therapy-speak.**
> "Most people aren't lazy. They're overloaded." → "They need another capable person." `— [BRAND-VOICE.md, lines 79–81]`
> — OBSERVATION: Your emotional architecture is stated as a 3-step: (1) notice reality, (2) remove shame, (3) introduce relief.

**7. Credibility by scope, not stats.**
> "I've spent a career being handed inventory worth millions and deciding what stays, what goes, what sells, what's broken, and what's worth fixing." `— [WLC-SITE-COPY-FINAL.md, line 111]`
> — OBSERVATION: Your voice-capture doc says "Numbers shrink her; the rooms she ran don't." You retired the "15+ years" framing. Lead with the caliber of what you ran, not a year count.

## SENTENCE RHYTHM (how it moves on the page)
— OBSERVATION, drawn from the samples:
- **Fragments are allowed and frequent.** "The move. / The storage unit. / The resale pile." You use line breaks as beats.
- **You repeat a frame to build a list** ("The room that… / The closet that… / The storage unit that…"), then break the pattern to land.
- **You undercut warmth with dryness** so it never gets sentimental — a tender line is usually followed by a flat one.
- **First person, plain.** "I come in, find the friction, and make the space work around your real habits."
- **You ask the question the client is actually typing** ("Is there a person who…can just help me get this handled?") rather than describing the client.
